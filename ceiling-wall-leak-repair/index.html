The Symptom.

A Stain That Keeps Growing.

A brown ring on the ceiling, a bulge in the drywall, or a musty smell in one bathroom wall are the calls we get most for this. By the time it's visible, the leak behind it has usually been running for a while, whether from a bathroom above, a roof issue, or a supply line inside the wall itself.

Our Method.

Find the Source, Then Repair

We use acoustic and moisture detection to trace the leak's actual source before opening drywall, since a ceiling stain doesn't always sit directly below the leak itself, water travels along framing before it shows. Once the source is fixed, drywall and paint repair complete the job.

Second-story bathrooms above a first-floor living space are the most common source of this call in Bellflower's older two-story homes, since a slow leak at the tub or toilet flange there shows up as a ceiling stain one floor down.

We use an infrared moisture camera in addition to the acoustic tools where the source isn't obvious right away, which maps the actual wet area behind the surface rather than guessing from the visible stain's edges.

Once the source is confirmed, we open only the section of drywall that actually needs it, not the whole wall, which keeps the repair and repaint smaller than most homeowners expect going in.

If You Suspect Mold.

What to Do Before We Arrive

Do Not Disturb the Area

Avoid scrubbing, painting over, or cutting into visible mold, which can release spores into the air.

Limit Access to the Room

Keep kids and pets away from a suspected mold area until it's assessed.

Reduce Humidity If Possible

Run a fan or dehumidifier nearby without directly disturbing the affected surface.

Call Us for an Assessment

FAQs.

Common Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover ceiling or wall leak repair?

The plumbing fix itself is usually not covered, since it's treated as maintenance on the source of the leak. Water damage to drywall, insulation, or flooring that resulted from a sudden leak is often covered separately, though coverage details vary by policy.

Is the leak always directly above the ceiling stain?

Not always. Water travels along framing and joists before it shows, so the actual source can be a few feet away from where the stain appears.

Should I be worried about mold behind the wall?

It's worth checking, especially if the leak has been running for a while. Drywall that's stayed wet for more than a day or two is a real mold risk.

Will you patch the drywall after fixing the leak?

Yes, drywall and basic paint touch-up are part of the repair, so you're not left with an open wall after the leak's fixed.

Can this wait a few weeks before I get it looked at?

We'd recommend against it. A slow leak left alone gets more expensive the longer it runs, both in plumbing and in the surrounding materials.

Could this actually be a roof leak instead of plumbing?

Possibly, especially if the stain is on a top-floor ceiling near an exterior wall. We check for both, since roof and plumbing leaks can look nearly identical from inside.

What's the actual cause of a musty smell before any stain appears?

Moisture trapped behind drywall or in insulation before it saturates the surface enough to show a visible mark. Trust the smell as an early warning even without a stain yet.

Do you repair the paint finish, or just the drywall patch?

Basic paint touch-up matched to the existing wall is included. A full room repaint to blend a larger patch perfectly is a separate conversation if the room is highly visible.

Is it normal for a repaired spot to look slightly different in color?

A close color match is standard, but paint can fade unevenly over time, so a patch on an older wall sometimes shows a faint difference until the whole wall is eventually repainted.

Should I turn off the water while waiting for the appointment?

If the source is a fixture you can isolate, yes. Otherwise a small ongoing leak is generally safer to leave running until it's traced than to guess and shut something off blind.

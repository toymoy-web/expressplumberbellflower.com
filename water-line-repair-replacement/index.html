Water Line Repair & Replacement in Bellflower, CA

Repair or replacement for the main service line between the water meter and your house, traced and fixed without guesswork.

The Symptom.

Meter Spinning, Water Bill Climbing.

The water line between your meter and your house is the one supply run most homeowners never think about until it fails. The usual first sign is a water meter that keeps moving even with every fixture in the house shut off, a soggy patch in the yard between the meter and the house, or unexplained low pressure throughout the property.

Meter Running With Everything Off

The strongest single sign of a leak on the service line itself.

Wet or Sunken Spot in the Yard

Water surfacing along the path between meter and house.

Low Pressure Throughout the House

Rather than at just one fixture, pointing to the main line, not internal plumbing.

Unusually Green Grass in One Strip

A leaking line is effectively watering that section of the yard.

Any one of these on its own is worth a call. The meter test is the most reliable, though: if it moves with every fixture off, something is using water somewhere on the property, and the service line is one of the first places we check.

A line failing in one spot after another is a sign the whole run may need full replacement rather than repeated patches. If the meter keeps moving but you can't find where, our leak detection service can trace it precisely before anyone starts digging.

Our Method.

Trace First, Then Repair.

We confirm the leak is actually on your side of the meter, since a leak between the street and the meter is the water provider's responsibility, then use acoustic detection to pinpoint the location along the run before any digging starts. Depending on the pipe material and the location, we can often repair or replace a section without trenching the entire yard.

Older copper and galvanized service lines are the ones most likely to fail outright rather than develop a small leak, so once one section has gone, we check the rest of the run's condition before calling the job finished.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover water line repair?

It depends on the cause. A sudden break is sometimes covered as accidental damage, while a line that failed gradually from age or corrosion is typically treated as wear and usually excluded. Check your specific policy for the distinction.

Whose responsibility is the line, mine or the water company's?

Generally, the property owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house. The water provider is responsible for the line up to the meter. We help confirm exactly where a break falls.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not usually. Acoustic tracing narrows the location first, and many repairs are done as a targeted dig rather than trenching the full run.

How long does a water line repair take?

Most spot repairs are completed in a single day. A full line replacement can take one to two days depending on length and access.

Can this cause my water bill to spike?

Yes, a leaking service line runs through your meter continuously, which is often what triggers a homeowner to call in the first place.

What's the difference between this and a slab leak?

A water line leak happens outside the house, between the meter and the foundation. A slab leak happens under the house itself. Both can raise your water bill the same way, so we trace first to confirm which one it actually is.

Do you need to shut off water to the whole house during the repair?

Yes, briefly, since the repair is on the line feeding the entire property. Most repairs are completed within the same day so the water is only off for part of a visit, not overnight.

Can a water line leak damage my foundation over time?

It can, if it's left running long enough. Saturated soil next to a foundation is part of what makes this worth fixing promptly rather than waiting to see if the bill goes back down on its own.

Do you replace the whole line or just the damaged section?

It depends on the pipe's overall condition. A single failure on an otherwise sound line usually gets a spot repair. A line showing multiple problems is often better replaced in full rather than patched repeatedly.

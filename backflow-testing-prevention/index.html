What This Covers.

Why a Backflow Test Might Land on Your Property

Bellflower properties don't get backflow-testing letters at random. It comes down to whether your connection creates a cross-connection hazard, meaning a path where non-drinking water could flow backward into the public supply, that the local water purveyor is required to guard against under state plumbing regulations. Once a property is flagged, the requirement doesn't go away on its own. The assembly has to be tested and the paperwork filed every year the connection exists.

Irrigation Systems

A dedicated irrigation meter or a system with fertilizer/chemical injection is the most common residential trigger.

Fire Sprinkler Systems

Commercial and some multi-family fire suppression connections require an assembly and annual testing.

Commercial Connections

Auto shops, medical and dental offices, and food service typically carry a higher hazard classification.

Pools & Auxiliary Supply

A property with its own well, pool fill line, or secondary supply can also trigger the requirement.

The Regulation.

What California Actually Requires.

California's Title 17 regulations, Sections 7583 through 7605 of the California Code of Regulations, require an approved backflow prevention assembly wherever a cross-connection hazard exists, and require that assembly to be field-tested at least once a year by someone competent to test it. The exact hazard classification and testing frequency for a given property is set by the local water purveyor, not by the plumber. That's why Bellflower's testing letters come from BSMWC, Home Garden, Cal-Am, or Liberty Utilities depending on which one serves your address, not from us.

What we do is the part after that letter arrives: field-test the assembly under pressure, repair or replace it if it fails, and submit the compliance report to your water provider or the county so the paperwork is actually closed out, not just the valve. Bellflower has four separate water providers depending on the address: Bellflower-Somerset Mutual Water Company, Bellflower Home Garden Water Company, California American Water, and Liberty Utilities. Each one runs its own notification schedule, so the deadline on your letter is specific to your utility, not a citywide date.

FAQs.

Common Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover backflow testing or assembly replacement?

No, this is regulatory compliance and routine equipment maintenance, not damage repair, so it's not something a standard homeowners policy covers. It's billed directly, and we always quote it before scheduling.

My water company sent a testing notice. What do I actually need to do?

Call us with the notice in hand. We confirm the assembly type it's asking about, test it, and file the results with whoever sent the notice.

How often does an assembly actually need testing?

Annually, at minimum, under Title 17. Some water providers require it more often for higher-hazard commercial connections.

What happens if my assembly fails the test?

We quote the repair or replacement on the spot. Most rebuild kits are completed the same visit.

Do I need this for a normal residential irrigation system?

If your irrigation runs off a dedicated meter or has a chemical injection setup, likely yes. We'll confirm against your specific water provider's rule before you pay for anything.

Can I test the assembly myself?

No. Title 17 requires the test be performed by someone competent to test it, and your water provider generally requires the results to come from a certified tester, not the property owner.

What if I ignore the testing notice?

Most water providers escalate to a shutoff notice after repeated non-compliance, since the whole point of the program is protecting the shared drinking water supply. It's cheaper and simpler to get it tested than to let it go.

Do I need a separate assembly for a fire sprinkler system?

Usually yes, and it's typically a larger, more expensive assembly than a residential irrigation setup. Commercial properties with fire suppression are one of the higher-hazard categories under Title 17, so testing tends to be checked more closely.
